### Summary

The corrections are mostly sound and several are genuinely load-bearing — the `AC6.2.3` prefix assertion is the cheapest high-value check in the set, `AC1.1.5` is decidable today against the armed `offline_guard`, the `AC7.7.2` hoist is verified necessary by `tests/conftest.py:102`, and the `US6.5` addition closes a real hole (the design artifact's Screen 3 and Screen 5 exist precisely because nobody owned them). But the corrections did not hold together everywhere: the widened thin slice still cannot run end to end (R-03), the `mean_confidence` simplification dropped its rounding rule (R-06), the accessibility split is nominal (R-11), and three upstream defects this stage proved false are still stated as fact in `requirements.md` (R-08). Two criteria are in direct contradiction — one on series emptiness for a bounded range (R-01), one on an envelope member the frozen shape forbids (R-02) — and each would send a developer and a QA author to opposite implementations.

On the count, which the human ruled on knowingly and which I am not reopening: 35 stories for a single-developer feature is not a licence to copy. Most carry a distinct decidable obligation, the dependency graph is honest about its two test-after cases, and the duplication is low. But it is not yet demonstrably a working backlog either, and the tell is specific rather than stylistic: two `AC` ids assert nothing (R-20), the story map and the dependency summary were never updated after the corrections (R-15), and `traceability.json` was not regenerated after the merge, so three requirements are owned by a story id that no longer exists (R-07). The overhead is justified; the bookkeeping that would justify it is not current.

The two Critical findings and the eleven Major ones are the ones I would weigh before approving. Everything else is cheap to fix alongside them.
